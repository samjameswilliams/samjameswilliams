# 6

This chapter begins with the account of the miracle of the feeding of the five thousand. The crowds see that Jesus didn't go in the boat across the sea to Capernaum with his disciples. Jesus goes to his disciples by walking on water to them in the storm, in secret, and called the storm. Only the disciples saw him do this. 

So the next morning the crowds are confused about where Jesus has gone but they catch up with him. 

At this point they're in the palm of his hand. They're trekking after him to hear his teaching and hoping for more miracles. Probably hoping too that he would lead them in revolt against the Romans. 

>$^{25}$When they found him on the other side of the sea, they said to him, “Rabbi, when did you come here?” $^{26}$Jesus answered them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, you are seeking me, not because you saw signs, but because you ate your fill of the loaves. $^{27}$Do not work for the food that perishes, but for the food that endures to eternal life, which the Son of Man will give to you. For on him God the Father has set his seal.”

It's quite notable that instead of saying "Well, actually I walked on the water to get here, and saved the lives of my disciples by calming that huge storm last night", he instead rebukes the crowd for following him for petty reasons. He could easily have lapped up their praise, impressed them some more and become the leader they wanted but instead he challenges them. 

>$^{28}$Then they said to him, “What must we do, to be doing the works of God?” $^{29}$Jesus answered them, “This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent.”

This is a good question to ask of Jesus, but the answer isn't what they were expecting. They probably want to do something grand and impressive. Just to believe in Jesus? What does he mean?

The exchange continues at crossed purposes. The crowd make an obvious hint they want to see Jesus make more bread and doing so would put him on par with Moses.

>$^{30}$So they said to him, “Then what sign do you do, that we may see and believe you? What work do you perform? $^{31}$Our fathers ate the manna in the wilderness; as it is written, He gave them bread from heaven to eat.’”

Jesus is determined not to be side tracked. He's trying to teach them about  their need for him. They ask about bread, so he introduces a metaphor calling himself "the bread of life".

>$^{32}$Jesus then said to them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, it was not Moses who gave you the bread from heaven, but my Father gives you the true bread from heaven. $^{33}$For the bread of God is he who comes down from heaven and gives life to the world.” $^{34}$They said to him, “Sir, give us this bread always.”
$^{35}$Jesus said to them, “I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger, and whoever believes in me shall never thirst. $^{36}$But I said to you that you have seen me and yet do not believe. $^{37}$All that the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never cast out. $^{38}$For I have come down from heaven, not to do my own will but the will of him who sent me. $^{39}$And this is the will of him who sent me, that I should lose nothing of all that he has given me, but raise it up on the last day. $^{40}$For this is the will of my Father, that everyone who looks on the Son and believes in him should have eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day.”

Initially the crowd seem to want the bread from heaven Jesus is offering. So Jesus reiterates that the bread he is talking about is himself. He then makes it explicit that he's offering eternal life. 

In this section Jesus doesn't flinch away from talking in ways that stress both God's control over everything, even us "All that the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never cast out."

You might well ask, if God gives people to Jesus, can they choose to believe? And conversely if people choose to believe, can God be in control? Paradoxically, the Bible teaches us that both things are true and possible at the same time, and it's not just true of people coming to faith in Jesus, but of everything. When Pharaoh refused to let the people go from Egypt the Bible talks both in terms of Pharaoh refusing as though it's his choice, and God hardening his heart.

Jesus uses his metaphor to explain that he is the answer to their greatest need. Believing in him means eternal life, being raised at the last day.

>$^{41}$So the Jews grumbled about him, because he said, “I am the bread that came down from heaven.” $^{42}$They said, “Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? How does he now say, ‘I have come down from heaven’?” 

The mood of the crowd has shifted decisively. Instead of playing to the crowd's excitement at seeing his miracles Jesus has upset them.

>$^{43}$Jesus answered them, “Do not grumble among yourselves.$^{44}$No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him. And I will raise him up on the last day.

Jesus draws attention once again to this mystery of how human free will and God being in control are not exclusive to each other. 

>$^{52}$The Jews then disputed among themselves, saying, “How can this man give us his flesh to eat?” $^{53}$So Jesus said to them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you have no life in you. $^{54}$Whoever feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day. $^{55}$For my flesh is true food, and my blood is true drink. $^{56}$Whoever feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood abides in me, and I in him. $^{57}$As the living Father sent me, and I live because of the Father, so whoever feeds on me, he also will live because of me. $^{58}$This is the bread that came down from heaven, not like the bread the fathers ate, and died. Whoever feeds on this bread will live forever.”

The message Jesus brings says no-one is acceptable to God, no-one can make up for their sins, our only hope is to believe in Jesus, to believe that he's ready and able to forgive us. This message is offensive to many people. People like to think their efforts, sacrifices, self control, or whatever else they've done is enough, God should be pleased with that. 

It is quite clear that Jesus is speaking metaphorically about being the bread of life. He even explains what it refers to. Nevertheless, the crowd dispute about the imagery rather than what it represents asking “How can this man give us his flesh to eat?” 

Instead of softening his message, or at least, making it more palatable, Jesus seems to deliberately emphasise the offensiveness of it. He repeats the necessity of feeding on his flesh, and drinking his blood in order to have life, eternal life, Jesus abiding in them and then in him. He does this even though he doesn't mean he's expecting them to actually cannibalize him. But the cannibalistic imagery is surely intended to get their attention. The Jews prided themselves on only eating foods classed as clean in the law of Moses. Cannibalism wasn't even practised by the pagans living around them.

The crowd would have thought they were pointing out the absurdity of Jesus' metaphor when they ask “How can this man give us his flesh to eat?” But Jesus chose this metaphor carefully and it's loaded with symbolism. Just as our bodies need food (symbolised by bread) to survive, our soul needs Jesus. Just like the grain from which bread is made is crushed to make flour, Jesus was crushed for us. As we break bread to eat it, his body was broken to give us life. Jesus comes back to the very same symbolism when he institutes the Lord's supper.

The other part of Jesus' message which caused offence was the claims of superiority. One is claiming to be greater than ordinary bread. That's not so offensive, except the crowd may have wondered what he meant that if they had the living bread they wouldn't hunger. The other claim was that Jesus was superior to Moses. This would have been a really big claim to make to the Jews to whom Moses was one of their most revered ancestors. But it is true and the Exodus can be seen as a foreshadowing of what Jesus would come to do. 

>$^{60}$When many of his disciples heard it, they said, “This is a hard saying; who can listen to it?” 

We aren't told the stories of anything like all the people present here. There were probably plenty of people who rejected Jesus out of hand forever. But there were probably others who initially  couldn't accept his teaching and him personally who later did come to believe. Nicodemus seems to have taken time. It's understandable that for a Jew who is devoted to serving God in the right way that they would be cautious of accepting Jesus' claims which involve leaving the old covenant behind. It's probably hard to understand just how big a leap this would be. It's easy in retrospect to say things like "they should have easily understood and turned to Jesus". But they would have been worried about being conned and by doing so disobeying God. Expectations about what the Messiah would be like would have varied. Even those most prepared are seen to be surprised by Jesus in numerous places. Thankfully, Jesus is patient with us and knows how to draw people to him.

>$^{61-65}$But Jesus, knowing in himself that his disciples were grumbling about this, said to them, “Do you take offense at this? Then what if you were to see the Son of Man ascending to where he was before? It is the Spirit who gives life; the flesh is no help at all. The words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life. But there are some of you who do not believe.” (For Jesus knew from the beginning who those were who did not believe, and who it was who would betray him.) And he said, “This is why I told you that no one can come to me unless it is granted him by the Father.”

John's comment that Jesus knew who did not believe and who would betray him really highlights Jesus' patience and how he really practices loving his enemies. The obvious person he is referring to is Judas who Jesus treated like all the other disciples, as a friend. 

>$^{66-71}$After this many of his disciples turned back and no longer walked with him. So Jesus said to the twelve, “Do you want to go away as well?” Simon Peter answered him, “Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life, and we have believed, and have come to know, that you are the Holy One of God.” Jesus answered them, “Did I not choose you, the twelve? And yet one of you is a devil.” He spoke of Judas the son of Simon Iscariot, for he, one of the twelve, was going to betray him.

From being ready to follow Jesus wherever he goes, many of his disciples leave Jesus after this. Jesus is ready to accept the consequences of doing his Father's will. We must be on guard against the temptation to look for results and popularity rather than to do good. The end doesn't justify the means. We must also guard against the temptation to cast ourselves as martyrs whenever we suffer, even when we're actually suffering justly for doing wrong. 

Jesus doesn't coerce people into following him. He asks his disciples directly if they want to go away too. 

Simon Peter's response is one that has come to mind many times when I doubt. “Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life, and we have believed, and have come to know, that you are the Holy One of God.”

I can't understand many things in scripture or that happen. But when I look around I feel the same, where else and to whom else would I go? And despite there being things I struggle with, when I read the word of God, the words of Jesus and think about what he is like, I agree, Jesus is the Holy one of God.
